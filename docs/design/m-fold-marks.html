Fold marks

Exploration aid — not a grammar change

One law, or three sites. Under the downstream law a mark folds everything downstream of itself, the branch head included, and the marks sit at departures: every elbow, the take-off's arm, and the trunk below the final segment. Under the current model a fold hides what lies below an owner and the owner stays, so marks sit at three sites — a segment's tee, a chain head's line, a fork tail's line. What each gives up: the downstream law loses run-skimming, because there is no mark below a chain head and folding Petja's branch takes Petja's post with it; the current model loses per-branch folding — Luke and Clifton are leaves and carry no mark at all — and loses the uniformity of a single rule. A third sketch sits under the current model as mid-run marks: a mark below every dialogue bead but the last, which buys “fold the rest of this exchange from anywhere” at the cost of a mark between every pair of beads. A third algebra, collapse to byline, is the Reddit / HN / LessWrong convention: folding a node hides its body and its whole subtree into its own identity row, bead and byline intact with the count riding the byline, and leaves fold too. In this mode the tee keeps the hide-below verb — a reply block has no single head to collapse into — which makes it the only mode on this page with two fold verbs at once. Below-avatar marks keep one station for both states — the open handle and the closed stub share an anchor, per K's take-off discipline — while departure marks get that for free, the mark already standing in the position the folded branch is removed from.

Always, or on approach. Every ⊖ can rest at line ink and brighten under the pointer, which makes the fold structure part of the drawing and puts a mark at every station whether or not anyone is looking for one; or the marks can be absent until the line they stand on is hovered or focused, which leaves the tree bare and makes a fold something you find rather than something you read. ⊕ stubs stay visible either way, because a closed fold has to be discoverable. Mid-run marks are where this toggle starts to bite: sparse marks tolerate being always visible, and a mark between every pair of beads argues for reveal. Whichever is showing, a fold is one control in three shapes: the mark, the line it stands on — click anywhere along a scope's rail and it folds, in every mode — and the chip that stands in for the content while it is closed.

Not being decided here: the ⊖ / ⊕ glyph pair and the stub's n · k new wording are shared by every combination below — the app's existing stub text in a new seat. The two questions are where marks sit and when they show.

Mark visibility
A mark folds everything downstream of itself, branch head included. A mark folds what lies below its owner; the owner post stays. A mark collapses a node to its own byline — except the two reply blocks, which keep the hide-below verb.
