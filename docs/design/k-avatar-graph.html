The grammar — every mark the tree can make

Seven marks, and the reader only has to learn one rule: the line bends only where the conversation branches. Everything else is the line continuing. Depth is charged at roughly 2rem per fork level and never for a run, which is why a twelve-post exchange between two people costs the measure nothing. The take-off is the one mark that governs the trunk: a spine segment's replies bundle onto a single sub-line so the trunk itself never has to bend, and “thread, or reply?” is seen rather than read. The seventh is the only mark that is also a control: the ⊖ / ⊕ fold station, which rests on the line at quiet ink whether or not anyone is pointing at it, and never moves between its two states.

┣━╮ Take-off = a segment's reply blockOne junction on the trunk, one sub-line, and the trunk runs clean past — one block for any number of replies, a single one included. The segment's fold station sits mid-arm, on the straight span between the two curves (s-amend) — never on a corner, and never under the avatar.
Straight line = continuationOne reply, so no elbow: the next avatar is the next bead, same indent.
┣ Elbow = fork, more below2+ replies. The spine passes straight through to the next sibling.
╰ Elbow = fork, last childThe only place a line is allowed to end is a corner.
Dashed circle = gapA post we never got. Its replies still hang below it.
⊖ / ⊕ = the fold stationA disc on the line where the hidden content attaches: ⊖ open, ⊕ closed, never moving between them. Closed, the line continues from the mark to the ghost chip along its own direction (s-amend): a vertical drop turns into a ╰, as here; a tee's horizontal arm runs straight on. Either way the chip stands where the first hidden avatar would have.
Ring = unreadState, not structure — the accent's first form, moved onto the node.

The take-off, expanded and collapsed — same anchor

Both blocks below hang from the identical junction on the trunk, and — rulings (s) and (s-amend) — from the identical station on it: the ⊖ on the arm above and the ⊕ below are placed by one rule that never names a state, standing mid-arm at the centre of the arm's straight span, so the disc cannot move when it is pressed. Expanded, the take-off turns into a sub-line and the replies elbow off it; collapsed, the arm runs straight on past the mark — no second corner, because the line only turns where geometry demands it — and the ghost chip stands at the end of it, in the seat the sub-line had. In both, the trunk passes straight behind and carries on to the next segment bead — the bundle is Segment.replies, and a segment fold hides the unit rather than a row. The mark is held hovered here, so the expanded block is showing its fold preview under ruling (e-settled): the arm, its departure curve, the sub-line and the two elbows that reach the replies' avatars are lit — one line, first level — and the trunk running past them is not. That is the whole argument of the take-off, said in ink, and it needs no boundary to say it: the trunk is not the block's line, so nothing brightens it. Nothing else on the drawing moves either — no surface is tinted, and the collapsed block below keeps its own ink entirely.

One control in three shapes — the mark, its line, the ghost

There is no caret column any more, and no caret: ruling (s) puts a ⊖ on the line where the foldable content attaches, and one rule still places every one of them. A chain head or a fork tail — like K. V. Stein below — sprouts its children under its own avatar, so the mark stands on the drop there. A spine segment does not: its replies attach at the tee, so its mark rides the middle of the arm, on the straight span between the departure curve and the corner (s-amend; see the specimen above), and the trunk under a segment's avatar carries no mark at all, because nothing foldable hangs there. Clicking the row still selects — the mouse speaks vim — and clicking the mark, the line it stands on, or (while closed) the ghost chip all do the same one thing, because the line resolves to the scope's own mark and defers to it.

K. V. Stein's mark is held hovered below, and the preview says exactly one thing, per ruling (e-settled): this is the line you have hold of. Her drop brightens, the mark on it brightens with the line it is made of, and with them the first level of the same line — the two elbows that turn to meet her children's avatars. Nothing beyond that. The folded child's ⊕, ghost and elbow keep their own ink, because they are that child's line and not hers; no surface is tinted anywhere; nothing above the handle moves; no word of prose changes; and the last sibling's unread ring stays accent — state and pointing must never be read as one thing. The preview ink is louder neutral, never the accent: the accent has exactly three forms and “you are pointing at this” is not one of them.

I just want it the way reddit does it. Just the first level is highlighted.” So the page ships (0), ruling (e-settled) — the scope's own line brightens, first level only, the mark on it brightens with it, and nothing else on the page changes — and (1), (2) and (3) stay here as the record of what was weighed and put down.
